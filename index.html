The idea behind it

I don't think AI replaces talent. I think it amplifies it.

I see this every day at work. I build and deploy RAG pipelines and AI systems for Fortune 500 media and entertainment companies, and the pattern holds no matter the team: the people who get the most out of AI aren't the ones who know the most about AI. They're the ones who already had something real, real judgment, real taste, a real point of view, and just needed a faster way to act on it.

My parents don't work in tech. They kept asking me, separately, whether AI was going to take their jobs, and I didn't have an answer that fit in a text message. So instead of explaining it, I started showing them: here's how you'd use it for an email you're dreading, for a letter you don't understand, for a conversation you've been putting off. Watching it actually click for them is what convinced me the amplifier idea holds up outside of enterprise software too, not just for consultants and engineers.

A favorite example of this: a musician who takes his own voice and layers it live, in real time, into full vocal harmony no single throat could produce alone. The raw talent was always there; the tool just let it fully out.

That's the lens I build through, at work and here. Every tool on this site (the Mood Wall, Signal, People Like Me, the guide itself) starts from the same question: what's someone already capable of, that a good AI tool could help them actually do? And the projects I build outside the guide (Global Explorer, GenAI, Stock Advisor, Festival Clash Resolver) are the same instinct pointed at problems I'm just personally curious about.
